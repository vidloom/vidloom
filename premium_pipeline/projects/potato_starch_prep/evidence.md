## 4. Scientific Grounding & Fact Sources
- **Mechanism**: When raw potatoes are cut with a knife, the blade physically shears open thousands of plant cells (parenchyma cells), liberating granules of free amylose and amylopectin starch onto the freshly exposed cut surfaces.
- **The Sticking Problem**: If unwashed, these free surface starch molecules gelatinize almost instantaneously upon contacting hot cooking oil or metal (between 60°C and 70°C / 140°F–158°F), acting as an adhesive polymer that welds the potato pieces to each other and to the cooking vessel, promoting burning and soggy steaming.
- **The Solution**: Rinsing cut potatoes under cold running water physically washes away the liberated free surface amylose while leaving interior starches intact for structure and subsequent even browning.
- **Reference**: McGee, Harold. *On Food and Cooking: The Science and Lore of the Kitchen* (Scribner, 2004), Chapter 6: Vegetables and Fruits — Potato starches and surface gelatinization.
