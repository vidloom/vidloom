### Candidate 3 (SELECTED): The Surface Starch Problem — Why You Must Rinse Cut Potatoes

---

## 2. Target Audience & Viewer Problem
- **Audience**: Home cooks who struggle with pan-fried, roasted, or sauteed potatoes sticking to the pan, tearing apart, or burning before crisping.
- **Viewer Problem**: Cooks often peel and chop potatoes and throw them straight into hot oil or a pan, only to have them weld to the metal or turn into a soggy, mushy mass.
- **Core Cause**: Slicing raw potatoes shears thousands of plant cells, spilling sticky, free amylose starch onto the cut faces. In hot oil or butter, that raw surface starch gelatinizes instantly into edible glue.

---

## 3. Promise & Original Contribution
- **Promise**: An observable, scientifically grounded explanation of why cut potatoes stick, and the exact physical rinsing habit that guarantees separated, clean potato surfaces.
- **Original Contribution**: Showing the actual physical progression from unwashed raw potatoes to thoroughly washed, separated cut chunks, explaining the cellular cause without fake recipe claims or synthetic crunch foley.

---

## 4. Why Someone Watches Through the Payoff
- The hook opens on raw whole potatoes entering the sink, confronting a universal cooking frustration: *"If your potatoes keep sticking to the pan, the mistake happened before they ever hit the heat."*
- The middle connects the knife blade to cellular damage and sticky surface starch.
- The payoff shows the final thorough cold-water rinse in the colander, showing the clean, starch-free potato wedges ready for drying, giving the viewer an immediate, actionable takeaway for their next meal.
